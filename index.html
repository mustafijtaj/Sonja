About the Book

Not a highlight reel. The truth.

Sonja Ann Collins was three days too young to start kindergarten. She cried at the window as the other children went without her. It was the first of many moments she would spend on the outside, looking in.

Raised in a household where love was shown through gifts instead of words, and shadowed by her mother’s addiction, Sonja learned early to survive first and ask questions later. That question followed her into nineteen years of her own addiction, into relationships that took far more than they gave, and finally down to a rock bottom that had, as she puts it, a floor.

On October 9, 2006, she hit it and stood back up. What follows is the story of a woman who went back to school at thirty-nine, built a treatment center with no grants and no outside funding, and now spends her life helping others find the way out she once found.

Introduction

Before You Read Another Word

I almost didn’t write this book.

Not because I didn’t have a story. I had too much of one. Nineteen years of addiction will give you that. So will growing up in a family that was supposed to be your safe place and wasn’t. So will loving people who took everything you had and left you emptier than before. So will waking up one morning and not recognizing the woman in the mirror, not being sure she was worth saving.

I didn’t write this book because I have it all figured out. I wrote it because I survived the part where I didn’t.

But before all of that, she was lost. This book is the story of what happened between lost and found.

Meet the Author

Sonja Ann Scott

CADCQMHPFounder & CEO, SAC DUI Counseling CenterDoctoral Candidate

Sonja Scott knows addiction from the inside. Before she became a counselor, a CEO, or a doctoral candidate, she was a woman fighting to survive. On October 9, 2006, she chose recovery. Twenty years later, she has never stopped choosing it.

She returned to school while carrying the weight of her past and earned an Associate of Applied Science, a Bachelor’s in Behavioral Health, and two master’s degrees. Today she is the founder, CEO, and Clinical Supervisor of SAC DUI Counseling Center in the Chicago metropolitan area, and through her nonprofit, Sonja’s Gift NFP, she walks alongside people rebuilding their lives.

Pain, Peace, and Purpose is her first book. Her message is simple and hard-won: no one is too far gone, and pain can become the doorway to purpose.
